# PERSON-SOCIETY + ISR SCHEMA

## Self and Society

- **Self (with claimed rights) → Person**
- **Society**
- Relationship between **self/person and society** is mediated through different social structures.

## Mediation

1. **Family**
2. **Friends**
3. **Masses**
4. **State**
5. **History**
6. **Market**

- These serve as forms of **mediation between person and society**.
- There is a **structure for all mediation**.
- Mediation is analyzed through the **ISR registers**.

## ISR Registers

### Imaginary

- Images and representations
- How we imagine ourselves, others, and society
- Construction of identity and sense of self
- Examples:
    - Filipino identity
    - National identity
    - Filipino anthem in the cinema
    - Representation of history
    - Stories and narratives
- The writing of history itself can be a form of imagery.
- Stories have sequences because they follow structures and conventions.

### Symbolic

- Subject to **rules, patterns, and structures**
- Rules and structures can constrain a person.
- Exists through language, relationships, meanings, and systems.
- Examples:
    - Family
    - Friends
    - Masses
    - State
    - History
    - Market
- Friendship has symbolic expectations and rules.
- Family has roles and relationships.
- The market operates through systems of value and exchange.
- AI can follow symbolic rules and patterns.
    - A simple prompt with a set of rules can produce an essay.
- **Moral compass** can be understood through symbolic systems.
- Masses:
    - DOST scholarship
    - Idea of paying forward to the masses after years of work.

### Real

- The **limit of the Imaginary and Symbolic**
- Anything that cannot be fully put into:
    - Image form
    - Rule form
- A **point of tension**
    - When something cannot be fully expressed through existing rules or representations
- Appears when something cannot be completely explained.
- Knowledge is not complete.
    - There is always something beyond what we currently know.
    - Knowledge cannot be completely completed.
    - If knowledge were completely complete, one would be like God.
- There may always be things beyond our perceived sight or understanding.
- Things may appear functional but still have flaws.
- Human relationships:
    - Relationships have flaws.
    - These flaws are part of being human.
    - A relationship can be imaginary and symbolic.
    - It also has limits that cannot be completely explained.
- **Filler to totalize**
    - Attempts to list or explain everything.
    - "Everything" can continually expand.
    - "Etc." indicates that the list can continue.
    - Similar to an asymptotic point in mathematics.
