# Behavior Modification

## 1. Reasons for Low Participation in Physical Activity

- **Not enough time**
- **Health reasons**
    - Asthma and other health conditions
- **Family obligations**
- **Lack of motivation**
- **Weather**
- **Lack of facilities or workout partners**
- **Negative attitude toward physical activity**
- **Lack of knowledge regarding physical activity**

---

## 2. Barriers to Change

- **Lack of core values**
- **Procrastination**
- **Preconditioned cultural beliefs**
- **Gratification**
- **Risk complacency**
- **Complexity**
- **Indifference and helplessness**
- **Rationalization**
- **Illusions of invincibility**

---

## 3. Motivating Yourself to Change

### Self-Efficacy

- **Self-efficacy** — belief in one's ability to perform a task.

### Sources of Self-Efficacy

- **Mastery experiences**
    - Confidence gained from successfully performing a task.
- **Vicarious experiences**
    - Seeing others succeed and thinking, **“If others can do it, I can do it too.”**
- **Verbal persuasion**
    - Encouragement and positive feedback from others.

---

## 4. Motivation to Change

### Motivation

- **Motivation** — the desire or willingness to do something.

### Locus of Control

- The figurative **“place”** a person designates as the source of responsibility for events in their life.
- Exists on a continuum:
    - **Internal locus of control**
        - Believing that one's actions influence life outcomes.
    - **External locus of control**
        - Believing that outside forces or circumstances mainly determine outcomes.
- People can develop a **more internal locus of control**.

### Impediments to Taking Action

- **Problems of competence**
    - “I don't know how.”
- **Problems of confidence**
    - “I don't think I can.”
- **Problems of motivation**
    - “I don't want to.”

# 5. Behavior Change Models and Theories

### 1. Learning Theories

- Behavior can be **learned through observation and experience**.
- People may imitate behaviors they observe in others.

### 2. Problem-Solving Model

- Behavior change involves:
    - **Identifying the problem**
    - **Finding possible solutions**
    - **Choosing a solution**
    - **Taking action**

### 3. Social Cognitive Theory

- Behavior is influenced by the interaction between:
    - **Personal factors**
    - **Behavior**
    - **Environment**
- **Self-efficacy** is an important component.

### 4. Relapse Prevention Model

- Focuses on **preventing and managing relapse** after behavior change.
- A setback does not necessarily mean complete failure.
- Helps individuals identify and manage situations that may cause relapse.

### 5. Transtheoretical Model

Behavior change occurs through **stages of change**:

**Precontemplation → Contemplation → Preparation → Action → Maintenance**

- **Precontemplation**
    - Not considering changing the behavior.
- **Contemplation**
    - Beginning to consider making a change.
- **Preparation**
    - Planning and preparing to make the change.
- **Action**
    - Actively making the desired behavioral change.
- **Maintenance**
    - Maintaining the new behavior and preventing relapse.

### 6. Spiral Model

- Behavior change is **not always linear**.
- A person can move **forward or backward between stages**.
- A person may **slip or relapse** and later return to action.
- Change is therefore viewed as a **continuous process rather than a straight path**.

---

# 6. Personal Factors

### Predisposing Factors

- Factors that make a person **more likely to adopt a healthy lifestyle**.

### Enabling Factors

- Factors that **help a person carry out a healthy lifestyle plan**.
- Include skills and resources that help people **follow through with behavior changes**.

### Reinforcing Factors

- Factors that provide **encouragement to maintain a healthy lifestyle**.
- Help people **stick with a behavior change**.
